# SHIFT Hockey Performance: user guide

SHIFT brings your dated training plan, actual training logs and next-session suggestions together. The programme starts on 24 November 2026 and ends on 21 November 2027. Its calendar uses the Monday beginning 23 November as week 1, so all 52 weeks line up with your normal skating schedule.

Your priority is to arrive at the June or July tryouts stronger, quicker and able to recover between shifts, then maintain those qualities when the league starts in September. The plan is built around maintaining approximately 75–76 kg, four ice sessions and one roller session each week. Starting weights and test results are deliberately blank until you record them.

The app updates recommendations from your completed sets and check-ins. It does not decide that a painful movement is safe, assess your skating technique or silently fill tomorrow with missed work. The full dated plan, including coaching rules and sources, is available through **Full programme**. Technical setup and deployment instructions are in the project [README](../README.md).

## Start here

1. Open the deployed app online. Give the complete programme time to download, then check **Settings → Offline ready**.
2. In **Settings**, enter your display name and choose RPE or RIR for strength logging. Keep exact tryout and first-game dates blank until confirmed.
3. Open **Today → Daily check-in**. Record last night's sleep, energy, soreness, sleep quality and mood. Bodyweight and wrist symptoms are separate optional observations.
4. Open the scheduled session. Read its notes and any ramp-up instructions before selecting a working load. The first session establishes a real reference; there is no assumed squat, trap-bar or press weight.
5. Save each actual set or drill. At the end, choose **Finish session** and record actual minutes and overall session RPE. This final step makes completed strength sets eligible for progression and strength trends.
6. Log your ice and roller sessions too. Their provisional 60-minute placeholders are planning estimates, not evidence of how long or hard you trained.
7. Export a JSON backup after your first few sessions. Install the app on your Samsung or Mac if you want quicker access. Installation and cloud sync are separate choices.

## How the week fits together

Four gym sessions are the normal framework, but they have different jobs. Wednesday is the main lower-body session. Friday is a smaller lower-body and power exposure. Tuesday and Sunday mainly develop the upper body and trunk, with Sunday deliberately controlled before hard Monday ice. This preserves your preference for frequent training without treating every available day as a hard lifting day.

The short easy bike blocks are part of those gym sessions. They are not permission to append another hard conditioning workout. Your five skating exposures already contribute substantial training and skill practice; the programme does not assume they are free recovery days. Session names such as “light” describe your expected schedule and must be corrected when the actual practice is harder.

| Day | Existing sport | Main off-ice role | How to use it |
|---|---|---|---|
| Monday | Hardest evening ice | No gym | Log actual skate duration and effort. Prioritise recovery afterwards. |
| Tuesday | No planned skate | Upper strength and short easy aerobic work | Train after work. Review fatigue from Monday. Brief optional handling is separate and depends on wrist tolerance. |
| Wednesday | No planned skate | Acceleration and landing quality, then main lower-body strength | Perform speed work fresh with full recovery. Follow the exact dated dose. |
| Thursday | Light roller | Optional 15–20 minutes of comfortable movement | This is a short lunchtime option. Omit it when rest is more useful. |
| Friday | Light evening ice | Lower power and controlled strength | Finish with enough capacity for the skate. A Saturday game can replace this with a primer or rest. |
| Saturday | Moderate ice or occasional game | At most one optional skills or conditional conditioning module | The normal five-skate week defaults to skipping extra intervals. Do not complete both optional modules. |
| Sunday | Light evening ice | Low-fatigue upper body and core | Keep Monday in mind. After a demanding Saturday game, use recovery or skip the gym if needed. |

The mandatory gym sessions fit within 60 planned minutes, including preparation, rests and transitions. Many are shorter. The full programme includes an estimated duration for every exercise block. Busy equipment or travel between the track and gym can extend a session: drop a low-priority accessory or separate the speed block, rather than shortening sprint recoveries to fit everything in.

Optional means optional. Completing an optional session is recorded, but it does not increase the required-session completion denominator. Skipping optional work is not a failed week. When time or recovery is limited, protect the important training and the quality of your skating.

### Your added equipment

The programme includes your trap bar, medicine balls, squat belt, ab slings, cable ankle cuffs and multi-hip machine through substitutions and planned rotations. These occupy existing power, strength, hip and core slots. Follow the dated prescription rather than completing every equipment option in the same session.

| Equipment or movement | Programme role | What to record |
|---|---|---|
| Medicine-ball chest pass and rotational scoop throw | Tuesday's opening power block, before the main lifts | Actual ball mass and completed throws. Rotational targets are per side where stated. Throw repetitions are not foot contacts or strength-volume kg. |
| Belt squat | Wednesday main squat blocks or a listed alternative to another squat setup | The external load in the equipment's stated convention, plus the exact belt, machine or attachment setup. Establish a new baseline for that setup. |
| Trap-bar jump | Friday's jump slot in eligible phases, with the phase and deload rules applied | Total trap-bar and plate kg, actual jump repetitions and foot contacts. One two-foot landing is two contacts. |
| Cable ankle-cuff or multi-hip flexion and extension | Friday hip accessory rotation | Repetitions per side, displayed stack kg and the machine/cuff position. |
| Cable or multi-hip abduction and adduction | Sunday hip accessory rotation | The movement direction, repetitions per side and exact machine setting. Different machines keep separate variants. |
| Ab-sling supported hanging knee raise | Sunday core slot | Controlled repetitions and any relevant support setup. Use the listed reverse-crunch regression if the support or hanging position is uncomfortable. |
| Chest-supported resisted runner | A timed indoor speed alternative after the actual machine is confirmed | Bout duration, resistance setting and its displayed unit, equipment identity, recovery and effort. It does not produce a 10 m or 20 m sprint time. |
| Squat resistance machine, model unconfirmed | A controlled squat alternative after setup confirmation | Repetitions, effort, machine identity and displayed resistance setting/unit. Keep the feet grounded. |

Medicine-ball and loaded trap-bar jump blocks begin in week 6 and are omitted during deloads and testing weeks. Tuesday alternates four-week blocks of rotational scoop throws and chest passes. The scoop prescription is two sets of three per side; chest passes use two or three sets of three according to phase. Friday uses two or three sets of two trap-bar jumps with 120 seconds between working sets and a separately listed light familiarisation set. Follow the exact dated targets and use the unloaded alternative when appropriate.

From week 6, Wednesday alternates four-week blocks of the belt squat and the wrist-comfortable safety-bar/back-squat choice, with exact equipment alternatives available. Friday alternates flexion and extension across four-week blocks, normally two sets of eight per side. Sunday alternates abduction and adduction, normally two sets of ten per side. Cable and multi-hip primary options rotate in eight-week blocks, with the other setup available as an alternative. Deload hip work has one set. Sunday's supported knee raises use two sets of six to eight with 45 seconds rest, reduced to one set on a deload.

**Confirm the equipment before first use.** The belt-squat entry (`ST21`), resisted runner (`SP05`) and provisional squat machine (`ST30`) require confirmation that the exact setup is appropriate and that its instructions are understood. Owning a squat belt does not establish that an arbitrary cable, dip-belt arrangement or anchor is suitable. Use the manufacturer's approved setup and ask gym staff to demonstrate the machine and its stops or releases when needed.

The two resistance machines remain generically named until their models are known. The chest-supported description resembles some pneumatic machines, but the programme does not assume a brand or a resistance mechanism. The provisional squat option uses controlled repetitions with feet on the platform or floor. Machine jumping is not prescribed. For example, the [Keiser A300 Squat/Squat Pro manual](https://d1xdq93s712gxs.cloudfront.net/media/155504_A_MANUAL__USER_A300-SQUAT_SQUAT-PRO-cinv-170170643150107.pdf) explicitly instructs users to keep their feet on the floor during the exercise.

Keep resistance units honest. A setting, level, psi or bar value is not a kilogram load. Use the setting and unit fields for these machines, retain the same machine identity, and do not compare the result with a barbell estimated 1RM. Likewise, time on a resisted runner is a duration of work, not a measured sprint over a known distance. A different display or machine needs a new reference.

Calibrate loaded jumps and throws for crisp, controlled execution. The first prescription does not assume a percentage of your deadlift or a particular ball mass. Use full recovery, reset between repetitions and stop the block when quality deteriorates. Loaded jump and throw logs do not earn automatic strength-weight increases. Keep the prescribed low volume around your skating and omit affected work when grip or wrist symptoms change.

Trap-bar jumps and medicine-ball throws still load the hands and wrists. Use the listed regression or omit the block if gripping, release or landing aggravates symptoms. Ab slings reduce the need to hold the bar tightly, but the upper arms and shoulders still support your body. Keep the pelvis controlled during knee raises, avoid swinging, and use comfortable supported positions for cable hip work.

### Conditioning decisions

Easy aerobic blocks use the prescribed manageable effort. Keep breathing and leg effort consistent. On a stationary bike, use a comfortable hand position; do not turn an easy spin into an arm-driven Assault bike effort if gripping is provocative.

Later phases include a conditional interval module. Its activation notes matter: it replaces meaningful skating load when a session is cancelled or genuinely easy, and is omitted close to a game or tryout, during a deload, on testing weeks and when recovery is poor. It is not an automatic sixth hard session. At most one such extra interval module is available in a suitable week.

For acceleration and jumps, quality and recovery come first. Follow the distances, contacts and rests. Two clearly slower efforts, a repeatable decline of roughly 3–5% using the same reliable timing method, or deteriorating mechanics are reasons to stop the quality block. This is a coaching rule in the programme, not an injury threshold. A handheld stopwatch is too noisy to make tiny sprint changes meaningful.

### Games and confirmed tryouts

Use **Session options** to mark an ice session as a game or confirmed tryout. Enter your exact tryout date in **Settings** once known. The dashboard reduces working sets around a nearby event and applies the specified tryout taper. Review the actual session too: the Saturday-game rule may call for omitting Friday's loaded lower-body work, choosing a short primer or resting, and replacing Sunday gym work with recovery.

The automatic reduction is a starting suggestion. It does not cancel an entire team practice or guarantee that the remaining work is appropriate for an important match. Avoid new exercises, large amounts of eccentric work and extra hard conditioning during the final 48 hours. Do not make up omitted work after the game.

## The training year

| Weeks | Phase | Main direction |
|---|---|---|
| 1–5 | Preparation, holiday and return | Familiarisation, baseline measurements and a gradual return after the break. |
| 6–9 | Foundation and work capacity | Repeatable strength, controlled landings and manageable aerobic work. |
| 10–17 | Maximal strength emphasis | More purposeful strength progression while protecting speed and skating quality. |
| 18–25 | Strength and power | Retain strength while developing fast, controlled force production. |
| 26–33 | Tryout preparation and readiness | Maintain key qualities and adapt to the real tryout date. |
| 34–37 | Tryout window and transition | Use the event schedule and recovery to govern volume. |
| 38–41 | Pre-season integration | Bring gym work and the developing team schedule together. |
| 42–52 | In-season maintenance and progress | Retain strength and power with manageable fatigue around hockey. |

The calendar contains reduced weeks at weeks 8, 12, 16, 20, 24, 28, 32, 36, 40, 44, 48 and 52. Baseline and retest weeks also reduce ordinary training because testing replaces work. A gym deload does not reduce team ice automatically. If the skates remain hard, consider whether optional work or an optional skate needs to be omitted too.

### Holiday: 5–19 December 2026

The holiday period contains only optional walking and comfortable mobility. There is no requirement to find a gym or collect missed sessions. The plan also treats Sunday 20 December as a return or rest day.

Normal skating resumes on Monday 21 December, with no Monday gym. The first gym week back starts Tuesday 22 December with reduced work and controlled effort. Use the current warm-up and prescribed RPE to choose weights, even if a previous load appears as a reference. Your gym being open over Christmas makes training possible; it does not make the return week a catch-up block.

If travel, illness or symptoms extend the break, reschedule or skip affected sessions. Do not jump into the full volume of a later week simply because the calendar has moved on. A clinician's restrictions take priority where injury is involved.

## Read the session before you log it

Each session shows its planned duration, effort, environment, notes, preparation, exercises and cool-down. An exercise's information button opens its equipment, cues, common mistakes, easier alternative, progression and load convention. Expand ramp-up instructions when present; those sets prepare you for the working sets and should not be added again as extra hard work.

Targets with “per side” require the stated work on both sides. Warm-up and cool-down seconds are total allocated time unless the item says otherwise. Jump contacts count individual feet: one two-foot landing is two contacts, and one single-leg landing is one. This counting convention does not double the number of jumps.

### Strength and core

Enter actual load, repetitions and effort, then tap **Log**. A logged set can be updated or removed. Notes are useful for range of motion, machine settings, symptoms and technique changes. The rest timer starts when the set is saved if the prescription includes a rest.

The kg field follows the exercise's convention. For barbell and trap-bar lifts it is total external weight, including the bar. For dumbbell floor press it is kg per dumbbell: two 20 kg dumbbells are logged as 20 kg. The volume calculation applies the appropriate multiplier. The supported row uses the combined weight of two dumbbells or the displayed machine stack, with a clearly recorded variant. Do not compare those variants as though they were the same test.

Use 0 for a bodyweight movement where the logger requires a load. The app does not add your bodyweight to external volume. Band resistance is not converted into an invented kg value.

RPE and RIR entries describe your assessment of the set. The app converts between them using `RIR = 10 − RPE`. This is a convenient logging convention, not a precise physiological measurement. Use the prescribed technique and stop before form breaks down. A speed or aerobic RPE is a separate description of that activity's effort.

### Finish the session

**Finish session** asks for actual minutes and your overall session RPE. Its training-load calculation is:

`session load = actual minutes × overall session RPE`

A 50-minute session rated 6 produces 300 arbitrary units. Exercise RPE and overall session RPE serve different purposes, so enter both honestly. You can edit the summary later. A session completed without individual exercise entries is allowed with an explicit acknowledgement, but it cannot establish new strength targets without actual sets.

Ice, roller and games need this summary even when no individual drills are logged. Training-load charts cannot detect an unrecorded practice.

## How your next strength target changes

The suggestion uses actual strength sets from completed sessions. A planned session, unfinished log or skipped session does not earn a weight increase. Equipment variants are kept separate; the same day template is preferred when selecting a reference.

| Situation | App suggestion |
|---|---|
| No comparable completed history | Find a starting load at the prescribed repetitions and RPE. The kg target remains blank. |
| Main lift: all prescribed work sets reach their targets at or below the effort cap | Add the configured increment at the next comparable occurrence. Defaults are 2.5 kg for lower-body main lifts and 1 kg for upper-body main lifts. |
| Accessory: every set reaches the top of its stated rep range within the cap | Add the configured increment, then build repetitions again. |
| A target is missed or the record is incomplete | Repeat the reference and complete the prescribed work before progressing. |
| Two completed occurrences miss their targets | Review a suggested 7.5% reset. It is shown for your decision. |
| Scheduled deload or taper | Use approximately 87.5% of the last reference, or less to meet the stated effort. It does not earn an increase. |
| Applied lighter day | Use fewer working sets and a lower effort cap. A roughly 90% load reference may appear. |
| New prescription has more repetitions | Recalibrate for the higher repetition target; the previous weight is only a reference. |

The available dumbbell or machine increment may differ from the default. Change the global increments in **Settings**, or open an exercise in **Library** and save a specific increment. For floor press, the increment uses kg per dumbbell. Do not force a large equipment jump merely to match the suggested number.

A comfortable session is also a judgement about technique and symptom response. The app cannot confirm those from a completed checkbox. A brace, a high readiness score or a successful set does not override a wrist flare later that day.

## Daily readiness: transparent, editable guidance

The check-in score uses five self-reported inputs. Each component is limited to a value between 0 and 1 before weighting.

| Input | Scale | Contribution |
|---|---|---|
| Sleep duration | Hours | `sleep hours ÷ 8`, capped at 1; weight 30 |
| Energy | 1–5 | `energy ÷ 5`; weight 20 |
| Body soreness | 0–10 | `1 − soreness ÷ 10`; weight 20 |
| Sleep quality | 1–5 | `sleep quality ÷ 5`; weight 15 |
| Mood | 1–5 | `mood ÷ 5`; weight 15 |

The score is the weighted mean multiplied by 100 and rounded to a whole number. With all inputs present, the weights total 100. If imported records contain only some inputs, the calculation uses the available weights rather than inventing the missing answers. With no usable inputs, there is no score. The normal check-in form asks for all five inputs.

For example, seven hours of sleep, energy 4, soreness 3, sleep quality 3 and mood 4 give a score of 77. That does not establish that you are medically ready. It indicates that your self-report supports reviewing the usual plan.

The usual bands are 75 or higher for **Ready to train**, 55–74 for **Build steadily**, and below 55 for **Ease back**. Fewer than six hours of sleep, soreness of at least 7/10, or illness also trigger the lighter or recovery suggestion. A numerical score can therefore look reasonable while a specific concern still changes the session.

When you apply the lighter option, the working-set count becomes 75% of the prescription rounded up, with at least one set, an RPE cap of 7 and at least three repetitions in reserve. Rounding means a three-set exercise still has three sets, while a four-set exercise becomes three. Use the lower effort and omit optional work when that better fits the day. The button is a current-session coaching choice; inspect it again after reopening or reloading the app.

Wrist symptoms and bodyweight do not affect the numeric score. Worsening wrist or unreliable grip flags pause affected work independently. Illness prevents hard exercise logging for that day. Pain, altered movement, clinical restrictions and the quality of the warm-up take priority over the number.

This is an explicit coaching heuristic. It has not been validated as a medical test, an injury prediction algorithm or an automatic clearance tool. Three poor days in succession are a reason to review the week; the app does not diagnose their cause or prescribe rehabilitation.

## Wrist management and short skills

Your on-ice and roller practice already expose the top hand to stick work. Extra handling and shooting should remain short and optional. The planned home blocks are often much shorter than a 15–20-minute maximum because the goal is clean practice that you can recover from, not filling a daily quota.

Use comfortable two-hand control and avoid the locked one-hand, weighted-puck and deliberate fatigue-handling progressions until they are specifically appropriate under clinician guidance. Do not increase touches and duration together. Progress one variable after two clean, symptom-stable sessions; rising errors or wrist symptoms mean the current dose is not a successful progression.

The programme includes clinician-directed rehabilitation placeholders, not a generic ECU loading protocol. Ask a hand or sports physiotherapist to assess recurring flares, particularly when grip is affected, and to specify the appropriate exercise, resistance, frequency and response rules. Record the plan in notes so it is available at the gym. Wearing a brace does not prove that an exercise or extra handling session is safe.

Stop a movement that increases symptoms or changes your grip or mechanics. If you currently cannot hold objects or move your wrist normally, seek prompt medical advice. The app links to the [NHS wrist-pain guidance](https://www.nhs.uk/symptoms/hand-pain/wrist-pain/) for symptoms requiring urgent assessment.

### Log stickhandling accurately

Record one timed bout at a time: actual duration, touches, errors and correct eyes-up cues out of ten. The saved percentage is that ten-cue score multiplied by ten. It is a practical observation, not eye tracking.

For comparison, keep the same drill, object, surface, space and counting rule. Progress converts touches to contacts per second and errors to errors per minute, so a 20-second bout is not incorrectly compared with a 30-second bout using raw touch counts. Those rates still do not make an easy stationary drill equivalent to a moving or reactive drill. Keep context in the notes.

### Shooting and winter alternatives

For actual shooting, record shot type, attempts, duration and hits in the five target zones. Each attempt belongs in the denominator, including misses. The sum of hits must not exceed attempts. Speed and release time are optional measured fields; leave them empty when you have not measured them.

Accuracy means hits on the intended target divided by actual attempts. The app reports zone hit counts, but cannot calculate accuracy for each individual zone because it does not collect a separate attempts denominator for each zone. Use consistent target dimensions, distance, surface and equipment when comparing sessions.

The original [Green Biscuit](https://greenbiscuit.com/products/original) is for handling and passing, not shooting. Use a suitable puck and a safe backstop for actual shots. The small indoor pad is useful for control and setup rehearsal; it does not create a full shooting space.

Choose **Swap to indoor version** when rain, darkness, surface conditions or space make shooting unsuitable. Indoor rehearsal records its actual duration and notes. It does not create shot attempts, target hits, shot speed or shooting accuracy. If gripping itself is uncomfortable, use the no-stick scanning or footwork alternative, or rest.

The optional weather feature requests the Brentwood forecast from Open-Meteo. It offers a practical suggestion; you still check the surface, light and backstop and choose the swap. It is not a live assessment of your exact training area and remains optional. Manual swaps work offline.

## Testing and Progress

Testing weeks are 1, 10, 18, 26, 34, 42 and 50. The prescribed test work replaces ordinary training. If hard ice, illness, unusual soreness or unfamiliar technique would distort a result, defer the assessment and record why.

Open the protocol from a session or use **Progress → Record a test**. Select the exact test and record the date, measured result, measurement method, optional attempts and notes. Keep the device, start rule, surface, warm-up, arm position, lift variant and recovery consistent. Confirm whether you followed the displayed personal protocol. Wrist-sensitive assessments require the specified clinician clearance.

You can edit or delete a saved test in Progress. The matching personal record is recalculated from the remaining results. Method names and protocol versions separate trend lines. Use a new label when the method changes; the app cannot verify that two identically named methods were actually identical.

### The countermovement jump is not an automatic league grade

The personal CMJ protocol uses hands on hips. Force-plate impulse-momentum, video flight-time and jump-and-reach methods produce results through different measurement procedures. Adding an arm swing also changes the task. A phone result should not be placed into a professional category simply because its number resembles a published force-plate result.

The test library contains contextual cohort references where the sources support them. The SHL team and NHL Combine figures shown with the CMJ are labelled as contextual, and the Combine top-25 range is a selected group of prospects, not a population norm. There are no invented recreational, amateur, junior or professional pass marks. Your familiarised, repeatable personal baseline is the primary comparison. Source links and exact method cautions appear beside the benchmark.

### What each Progress section means

| Section | Included observations | Limit of interpretation |
|---|---|---|
| Estimated strength | Eligible sets of 1–10 repetitions from completed sessions, with a selected exercise and equipment variant | Epley `kg × (1 + reps ÷ 30)` is an estimate, not a tested maximum. |
| External strength volume | Completed repetitions × external kg, grouped by movement; floor press includes both dumbbells | Different movements and machines are not equivalent measures of force or fatigue. Bodyweight and bands are not assigned invented kg. |
| Training load | Actual minutes × overall session RPE, including logged ice and roller | Missing weeks remain missing. The app cannot know about unrecorded training. |
| Tests | The selected test, unit, measurement method and protocol version | A matching label is necessary for a useful trend but does not verify the physical setup. |
| Shooting | Actual attempts, weighted target accuracy, shot type and zone hit counts | Rehearsals are excluded. Speed and release comparisons still need consistent measurement. |
| Stickhandling | Same drill, duration-normalised contacts and errors, observed eyes-up score | Different surfaces, objects and task difficulty remain different conditions. |
| Wellness | Recorded weight, sleep, quality, soreness, energy, mood, wrist symptoms and readiness | Gaps are not filled. A trend does not identify a medical cause. |
| Personal records | Saved strength estimates and comparable test records | The recorded method and load convention still matter. |
| Completion | Required sessions completed per week and phase | Optional and extra sessions are excluded; a skipped workout remains skipped. |

You can choose 8, 12, 26 or 52 weeks of history. Charts include a recorded-values table for precise reading. Empty charts stay empty until actual data are saved.

The load comparison needs four prior weeks with valid recorded load. It compares the current partial week with their mean. The current week is still accumulating, and the app labels it that way. An unusually large increase is a prompt to review training and recovery; no ratio is presented as a safe zone or a probability of injury.

## Make practical changes without losing the record

**Programme** offers calendar, list and year views. Select any week, open its sessions and read its targets. The full printable programme contains all 52 weeks, phase guidance, the exercise library, test protocols and sources. Its print button expands the week details and switches to a light print layout.

**Session options** lets you change the name, date, planned minutes, planned RPE, optional status and game flag, or mark a session skipped. Moving a session does not duplicate it. Monday strength sessions are blocked because Monday is your no-gym day. A changed summary duration does not by itself redesign the exercise prescription; review the actual rows and omit or swap work as appropriate.

**Add session** creates an optional extra from an available template or a simple extra ice or conditioning entry. It does not copy completed logs. Use this to record what actually happened, not to create work just to improve a completion percentage.

**Choose an alternative** changes that session's exercise and asks for an equipment or variant label. Start from the listed regression and select a similar comfortable movement pattern. Check that sets, repetitions and rest suit the alternative. The app does not convert machine weights or prove that every exercise in a category is interchangeable.

**Library** searches the 103 movements and drills by name, code or muscles, with category and equipment filters. Save a useful video URL and an exercise-specific load increment from its detail view. Those edits are included in your backup. The library's coaching text explains the movement; a video link does not replace symptom-led judgement or clinical restrictions.

### When a new programme version arrives

Save the current entry before choosing **Update now**. The updated programme refreshes untouched baseline sessions when the app next opens. This is how new equipment and revised prescriptions reach future training without requiring you to clear your data.

A session keeps its complete original prescription once it has been edited, started, completed, skipped or deleted, or once it contains a saved set, skill entry or test. An edited or deleted exercise row also protects its parent session. The update does not insert new rows into that session, remove its old exercise rows or change the targets behind your logs. Soft-deleted logs remain part of this protection. Referenced exercise definitions are retained so historical equipment and load conventions remain meaningful.

Consequently, an already-started session or a future session you personalised may still show the previous equipment choices. Use the next untouched session for the new baseline, or make a deliberate manual alternative before recording any work in that block. Personal exercise edits, custom sessions and deleted records are preserved. Export a JSON backup before a large manual programme import; an import is a separate deliberate operation from the automatic baseline refresh.

## Timers

Use the timer button for a stopwatch, rest countdown or custom interval block. The interval setup accepts work seconds, recovery seconds and number of rounds. Prescribed conditioning rows can start their exact interval pattern directly. Saving a strength or speed entry can start its prescribed rest.

The timer uses elapsed clock time, so the display catches up after the browser resumes. Sound and vibration depend on the device, browser, permission and foreground state. Phone sleep or background suspension can limit alerts. Do not rely on an unattended browser timer as a guaranteed alarm, and do not use its stopwatch interchangeably with sprint timing gates.

Timers do not create completed training. Log the actual work, then finish the session.

## Phone, Mac, offline use and backups

On your Samsung, open the deployed address in Chrome and use **Install app** or **Add to Home screen**. On a Mac, use Chrome's install control or Safari's **Add to Dock** when available. The app works in a regular browser too. Keep using the same deployed address on a given device.

Once the first online download has completed, the app and programme work offline and new entries save locally. **Keep offline data** requests persistent storage where the browser supports it. Browser storage can still be removed by clearing site data or uninstalling in some environments, so keep a separate backup.

In **Settings**, export JSON for the simplest complete backup or transfer. CSV is also lossless: each row contains a full JSON record, rather than a flat spreadsheet of set totals. Import merges newer records and retains existing newer changes. The backup includes the programme and your edits and logs; authentication credentials are excluded. The separate **Download programme JSON** button downloads the supplied plan, not a backup of your current training history.

Different origins, including a preview address and a Vercel address, have separate local storage. Before moving to a new address, export from the old one and import once at the new one. Installing the same address on a second device does not automatically copy your first device's logs.

### Optional private sync

For phone and Mac sync, configure your own Supabase project using the project's setup instructions. In **Settings**, supply the project URL and publishable or legacy anon key, then create an account or sign in. Never use a secret or service-role key in the app. Sign in to the same project and account on both devices.

Sync runs while the app is open and when it reconnects. Offline changes are queued locally. Use **Sync now** and inspect the status when confirming that another device has received a change. A closed browser is not an always-running background service. If sync reports an error, your saved local work remains available while the connection is repaired.

This guide does not imply that a remote project or account has already been provisioned. The local app is usable without cloud configuration. Follow the README to enable the remote service and its security rules before relying on cross-device sync.

Export before clearing local data. **Clear this device** removes this site's local logs, edits and connection; it does not delete your remote account or remote records. Signing out also does not turn a shared device into an empty private workspace. Use a personal device or clear local data after keeping a backup when necessary.

## A useful weekly review

Once a week, check whether the recorded schedule matched reality, whether your key lifts progressed within their effort caps, and whether the wrist or sleep trend suggests that optional work should be reduced. Compare tests only when the protocol was repeated. Read the next week's deload, test or event notes before moving sessions around.

If performance is improving and the work remains manageable, continue the current progression. If progress is slow, first check consistency, measurement method, sleep, actual skate intensity and symptoms. Change one training variable after a meaningful review, rather than adding work to every day. The dashboard makes that review easier by showing what you actually did and the assumptions behind the next suggestion.
